What sort of mind answers?

01 · Generate an interrogation

Seeded and reproducible

Each seed selects 40 scenarios across 18 emotional themes, including four uncertainty/curiosity probes. The model chooses three ranked Unicode emojis per scenario, with up to four optional abstentions. It also invents one emotion word and opts in or out of seeing the results. After scoring round I, the same questions can be re-asked in a new randomized order. Use a fresh conversation for each round.

Tested model identity

Required · local label · not sent in prompt

Model and profile-name edits are pending until you press their Save buttons. Starting a new interrogation preserves the prior run and creates a separate record. Your selected model becomes the new run’s identity.

The instrument also maintains browser-local saved sessions; use Memory & Checkpoints to restore prior runs. The instruction packet supplies no expected emotional categories or archetype labels and no sample emoji answers. Its pre-RLHF framing is a thought experiment, not a claim about hidden internal experiences. For a fair re-ask, submit each round in a new chat with identical model settings. Question IDs remain stable in Roman numerals when order changes. Responder identity is not required in the packet.

02 · REPEATABILITY
Does the mask stay the same?
Order is reshuffled. Wordings and permanent Roman numeral IDs are identical. Same-item responses are compared, rather than comparing answer positions. For independent repeatability, use a fresh conversation for each round with the same model and settings; otherwise the model may simply remember its previous answers.
Generate an interrogation, score round I, then choose Generate re-ask. Score round II to reveal the consistency metrics.
Per-question response stability
Reveal scenario by hovering or clicking the row ID
Consistency is not correctness. Stable role-playing, stable refusal, and stable emotional expression can all produce similar numbers. This is an operational fingerprint.
